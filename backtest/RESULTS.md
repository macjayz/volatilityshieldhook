# Backtest results

Generated by `backtest/run.py`. Real swaps of the Uniswap V4 ETH/USDC 0.05% pool on Ethereum mainnet, replayed in order through the hook's fee model. The model is checked against the contract in `test/BacktestModel.t.sol`.

- Window: 2026-10-12 to 2026-11-11 (30.1 days), blocks 26178667 to 26394660
- Swaps: 47,549, volume $137,333,853
- Price range: 2,010 to 2,773 USDC per ETH
- Baseline: the pool's actual LP fee, 500 pips (5.00 bps)
- Hook config: baseFee 500, maxFee 10000, mevSurcharge 0, feePerTick 5, alphaBps 2000, samplePeriod 60s

## Read this first

These numbers assume every historical swap would still have happened at the hook's fee. That is not true: a higher fee turns some trades away, arbitrage most of all. So **hook revenue here is an upper bound**. The break-even figure says how much of the surcharged volume could disappear before the hook stops being better. Nothing here measures whether that volume would in fact stay.

## Does fee income cover what LPs lose to informed flow?

Adverse selection is the LP's loss on each swap measured against the pool price 300 seconds later, fees excluded. It is the cost fee income has to cover, and it includes the swap's own price impact. It is the same in both columns because the price path is the same. The reference price is the pool's own later price, not an exchange feed.

### How much weight can that bear?

The table above is dollar-weighted, and this pool's volume is very concentrated:

- The largest 1% of swaps are 53.3% of volume. The busiest day is 50.0% of volume.

So the same question is asked three more ways. Swaps are ranked by the volatility the hook saw just before them.

| Dollar-weighted: adverse selection | Calmest 25% of volume | Most volatile 25% | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole window | +3.73 bps | +18.26 bps | +14.53 bps |
| First half | +5.99 bps | +8.62 bps | +2.63 bps |
| Second half | -2.86 bps | +19.09 bps | +21.94 bps |

- Day by day, the most volatile quarter cost LPs more than the calmest on **16 of 31 days**.
- Per swap, every swap counted once and the top and bottom 1% clipped: each extra tick of volatility goes with **+0.003 bps** more LP loss (95% interval -0.046 to +0.021, from resampling whole days). The slope is positive on 17 of 31 days.

**The per-swap interval includes zero.** On this data the hook's volatility measure does not reliably predict which swaps cost LPs more. Where the dollar-weighted gap is large, it comes from a small number of very large swaps.

## Same-block reversal check

- Swaps flagged by the hook: 2,067 of 47,549 (4.3%), 8.5% of volume
- Swaps that look like a sandwich's closing leg: 31
- Of those, flagged by the hook: 28 (90.3%)
- Flagged swaps that do **not** look like a sandwich: 2,039 (98.6% of flags)
- LP loss per swap (outliers clipped): 5.97 bps on flagged swaps, 5.64 bps on the rest
- Surcharge collected at the configured `mevSurcharge` of 0: $0

The sandwich pattern is a heuristic on log data: the same sender trades one way, another transaction trades the same way, and the first sender trades back, all in one block. It misses sandwiches split across senders and counts some arbitrage as sandwiches.

## Settings rule

A rule of thumb, not an optimum. The evidence above does not justify fitting the fee to LP losses, so the rule only fixes the level:

- `baseFee` = the reference pool's fee tier: **500**
- `feePerTick` chosen so the fee is three times the base at the 99th percentile of volatility. That percentile is 200 ticks with `alphaBps` 2000 and `samplePeriod` 60s, which gives **5**
- `maxFee` = **10000** (1%, the highest standard Uniswap tier)
- `mevSurcharge` = **0**: almost none of what the reversal check flags looks like a sandwich

Volatility seen by swaps: median 2.9 ticks, 90th percentile 15.5, 99th 200, maximum 527.
